# Human-writing and anti-slop editorial pass

The goal is not to beat AI detectors. The goal is credible authorship: a real
argument, concrete reasoning, natural cadence, useful specificity, and prose
that does not exhibit clusters of predictable model-written patterns.

Humanization comes from better editorial choices, not fake mistakes or random
imperfection.

## Pattern-density warnings

One natural occurrence can be fine. Rewrite clusters of:

- stock AI vocabulary and inflated corporate language;
- generic scene-setting and throat-clearing;
- `not X but Y` binary contrasts used as a rhythm crutch;
- faux-insight setups such as `what most people miss`;
- dramatic colon reveals;
- trailing `-ing` clauses that pretend to explain significance;
- importance puffery;
- vague attribution such as `experts agree` or `studies show`;
- fake-strong verbs where a concrete/simple verb is clearer;
- synonym cycling;
- negative-listing theatrics;
- stacked punchy fragments;
- repeated rhetorical setup/answer pairs;
- generic headings and gratuitous summaries;
- excessive bullets or decorative formatting;
- decorative em-dash clusters;
- excessive three-item rhetorical lists;
- paragraph/section symmetry created for presentation rather than reasoning;
- sycophancy, generic optimism, and empty praise.

High-risk vocabulary includes, but is not limited to: `delve`, `pivotal`,
`underscore`, `leverage`, `utilize`, `seamless`, `robust`, `landscape`,
`foster`, `nuanced`, `multifaceted`, `transformative`, `comprehensive`,
`holistic`, `realm`, `tapestry`, `elevate`, `unlock`, `game changer`,
`paradigm shift`, `best-in-class`, and `ever-evolving`.

Do not replace one polished cliché with another. State the actual mechanism or
fact.

## Cadence and predictability

Look for repeated fingerprints such as:

- `This is where...`;
- `The problem is...`;
- `The reason is...`;
- `That matters because...`;
- `The result is...`;
- `In practice...`;
- `At the same time...`;
- repeated claim -> explanation -> example -> takeaway architecture;
- every section starting with a thesis sentence;
- headings sharing the same grammatical form;
- repeated three-part structures;
- uniform paragraph lengths;
- sequences of pseudo-dramatic short sentences.

Ask whether the next sentence is repeatedly the most obvious LLM continuation.
If so, vary the reasoning architecture rather than merely swapping words.

## Cliches of thought

Remove polished but informationally empty propositions such as:

- `Technology is only as good as the people using it.`
- `AI will not replace people; people using AI will.`
- `The future belongs to companies that adapt.`
- `There is no one-size-fits-all solution.`

Keep familiar propositions only when the article gives them a specific,
genuinely new interpretation.

## Opening

Reject generic openings such as `In today's rapidly evolving...`, `Over the past
few years...`, `It is no secret that...`, and `Artificial intelligence is
transforming...`. Reject unsupported engagement bait such as `Everyone is wrong
about X`.

Prefer a precise claim, concrete observation, useful tension, surprising
verified fact, mechanism, or contradiction. Begin where the piece becomes
interesting.

## Ending

Do not recap the article. Avoid `In conclusion`, `Ultimately`, `As we move
forward`, `The future is clear`, `Only time will tell`, manufactured aphorisms,
and faux-profound mic drops.

End on the strongest legitimate consequence, implication, decision,
recommendation, unresolved tension, or concrete evidence. Then stop.

## Headings and formatting

Use headings only when they aid navigation or argument. Avoid generic labels
such as `Why This Matters`, `The Challenges`, `Looking Ahead`, `Key Takeaways`,
and `The Bottom Line` unless they are genuinely the clearest label.

Lists are for enumerable material. Do not convert prose into bullets for visual
variety.

## Read-aloud pass

Repair lines that sound like a presentation voice-over, contain too many balanced
clauses, stack abstract nouns, force unnatural pauses, sound more polished than
the publication warrants, explain what the audience already knows, or sound
written to impress rather than communicate.

Do not make every sentence short. Choppy uniformity is another synthetic
pattern.
