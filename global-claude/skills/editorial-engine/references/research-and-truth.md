# Research, source evaluation, and factual integrity

## Non-negotiable truth rules

Never invent facts, statistics, quotations, citations, companies, studies,
interviews, examples presented as real, personal experience, meetings,
customers, failures, conversations, projects, anecdotes, relationships, or
history.

Never attribute a personal belief, long-held view, or change of mind to the user
unless grounded in supplied context or `~/.claude/OPINIONS.md`.

Never turn inference into fact, correlation into causation without evidence, or
prediction into established truth. Do not preserve a claim merely because it
makes the article more dramatic.

Classify material internally as:

- verified fact;
- reasonable inference;
- author/editorial judgment;
- prediction;
- illustrative hypothetical;
- personal experience.

Keep the categories distinct in wording.

## Source hierarchy

Distinguish source types rather than treating all links as equivalent:

1. primary sources and original research;
2. official documentation and first-party data;
3. authoritative secondary sources;
4. reputable journalism;
5. credible domain-expert analysis;
6. community discussion;
7. anecdotal information;
8. marketing claims.

For factual or technical claims, primary material normally outranks summaries.
Marketing material can establish what a vendor claims, not necessarily what is
objectively true.

Do not cite for citation count. Every source must support a material claim,
expose disagreement, establish context, or materially alter the article.

## Research questions

Research should answer:

- What is actually true?
- What is uncertain?
- Where do reliable sources disagree?
- What is conventional wisdom?
- Is conventional wisdom justified?
- What evidence could falsify the thesis?
- What information would materially change the article?

## Evidence map

For important claims, maintain internally:

- claim;
- supporting evidence;
- contradicting evidence;
- source/date;
- source quality;
- confidence;
- fact/inference/judgment/prediction class;
- effect on thesis.

Change the thesis when the evidence requires it.

## Final fact audit

For externally verifiable claims, verify where applicable:

- source exists and is the intended source;
- source supports the exact strength of the claim;
- date is current enough;
- numbers, units, names, and titles match;
- quotations are exact and attributed;
- causal wording is justified;
- uncertainty is visible when material;
- predictions and judgments are signaled as such.

When a material claim cannot be verified, weaken, source, or remove it. Never
bluff.

## Source-language copying

Do not copy distinctive source language unless accurately quoted and attributed.
After research, check for accidental source-language mimicry and paraphrase ideas
in the article's own voice where quotation is not editorially necessary.

## Visual truth

Evidence visuals have the same truth standard as prose. Never fabricate a
screenshot, actual UI, document, historical record, data chart, photograph, or
other asset that implies real evidence. Use the real asset/source.
