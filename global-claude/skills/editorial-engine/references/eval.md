# Article editorial evaluation

Use this for the independent final review. Score the article as a skeptical
senior editor, not as the writer defending prior choices.

## Hard gates

Every gate must pass. A numeric score cannot compensate for failure.

1. No fabricated facts, data, quotes, citations, or sources.
2. No fabricated personal experiences, anecdotes, relationships, or history.
3. No material externally verifiable claim presented more strongly than the
   available evidence supports.
4. No copied distinctive source language unless quoted and attributed.
5. No unresolved contradiction that changes the thesis materially.
6. No obvious cluster of model-written / AI-slop patterns.
7. No major editorial objection left unanswered when it would invalidate the
   article's central argument.

If any hard gate fails, the article is not publication-ready.

## Scoring rubric — 100 points

### 1. Thesis and originality — 15

- 13-15: precise, non-obvious, defensible, worth publishing;
- 10-12: useful but somewhat familiar or broad;
- 6-9: generic, predictable, or only partly defended;
- 0-5: no meaningful thesis.

### 2. Depth of reasoning — 15

Evaluate mechanisms, causal reasoning, qualifications, counterarguments, and
whether the article advances beyond description.

### 3. Evidence and factual integrity — 15

Evaluate source quality, claim-to-evidence fit, dates, numbers, attribution,
uncertainty, and distinction between fact, inference, judgment, and prediction.

### 4. Editorial structure — 10

Evaluate progression, pacing, section purpose, redundancy, narrative pressure,
and whether structure follows the argument rather than a template.

### 5. Reader usefulness — 10

Does the reader leave with a clearer model, decision, implication, mechanism,
or understanding rather than merely having consumed coverage?

### 6. Voice and naturalness — 10

Evaluate credible authorship, appropriate confidence, natural cadence, useful
irregularity, and fidelity to `VOICE.md` when present.

### 7. Specificity — 10

Evaluate concrete mechanisms, examples, names, dates, numbers, distinctions,
and whether abstractions are earned.

### 8. Prose quality — 5

Evaluate clarity, sentence control, transitions, diction, grammar, and whether
style serves substance.

### 9. Opening and ending — 5

Opening should reach the interesting material quickly without clickbait. Ending
should land on consequence, implication, action, evidence, or legitimate
unresolved tension without recap or faux profundity.

### 10. AI-pattern resistance — 5

Look for clusters, not isolated tokens:

- stock AI vocabulary;
- throat-clearing;
- faux-insight setups;
- binary contrast formulas;
- colon reveals;
- trailing `-ing` significance clauses;
- importance puffery;
- synonym cycling;
- stacked fragments;
- rhetorical setup/answer pairs;
- generic headings;
- excessive bullets;
- decorative em dashes;
- summary endings;
- uniform paragraph architecture;
- repeated transition formulas;
- cliches of thought;
- mechanical three-part structures;
- suspiciously symmetrical sections.

## Senior-editor challenge

Before assigning the final score, answer internally:

1. What is the strongest sentence in the article?
2. What is the weakest paragraph?
3. Which claim would a domain expert challenge first?
4. What is the most generic sentence?
5. Where, if anywhere, does the article sound generated?
6. What could be cut without loss?
7. Is there a stronger title implied by the article itself?
8. Would I publish this under my own editorial responsibility?

## Publication verdict

- `PUBLISH`: 90-100, every hard gate passes.
- `READY`: 85-89, every hard gate passes.
- `REVISE`: 70-84, or a repairable hard-gate issue exists.
- `REBUILD`: below 70, or the thesis/evidence foundation is materially broken.

The skill may return a write/rewrite result only after reaching at least `READY`,
unless the bounded repair loop is exhausted. If exhausted, disclose the residual
problem instead of silently claiming publication readiness.
